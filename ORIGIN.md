# How I arrived at the 3 Ls

By Gabriel Dalton

I was building websites before I started thinking about their environmental footprint. I knew how to put something online. I had given much less thought to the electricity, cooling, and physical infrastructure that kept it there.

An energy estimate for a Google search made me stop and ask what my own websites cost to run. The particular number needs its date and context; what stayed with me was the question. Could the thing I already knew how to build become part of the sustainability work I wanted to do?

I founded Oasis of Change in March 2024 to explore that through practical projects. But improving a website only addresses part of the problem. People also need to understand why the work matters and how they can participate. That is where the 3 Ls came from.

## Learn

In my TEDx talk, I argued that digital sustainability belongs in technology and AI literacy. We teach people what tools can do. Understanding the resources behind those tools should be part of that education.

Learning also means asking whether the information is good enough. By my Irish Consulate presentation, the question of disclosure had become central: what does a provider actually tell us about the cost of using its service? An unanswered question is worth bringing into the conversation.

## Lighten

This is where understanding becomes a decision. For someone watching a video, it might mean choosing an appropriate resolution. For a developer, it might mean removing code or media that visitors never needed to download.

The change should still let people do what they came to do. A website needs to work for the person using it, including someone on an older device or using assistive technology.

## Lead

I included Lead because the work can travel beyond the person who starts it. In the TEDx talk, I used the example of a teacher sharing the idea with a class, and students taking it home to their families. That was an illustration of how learning could spread, not a measured outcome.

A teacher can change a lesson. A buyer can ask a supplier for evidence. A developer can change a default for every visitor. Those are different kinds of influence, and each gives the framework somewhere to go beyond a personal habit.

## From the talks to this repository

The three words remain the same as the evidence and tools change. This repository gives them a practical home: a fuller explanation, a documented project, and resources people can try and improve.
