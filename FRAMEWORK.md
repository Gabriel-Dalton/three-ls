# Learn, Lighten, Lead

The 3 Ls are a framework for making more intentional digital choices and helping others do the same. Each step produces something you can use in the next: understanding, a practical change, and shared learning.

## 1. Learn

Understand that online actions have environmental consequences. Ask what a service needs to run, what its provider measures, and whether an alternative could meet the same need.

Useful questions:

- What am I trying to accomplish with this tool?
- What devices, networks, and servers does it depend on?
- Does the provider publish energy, emissions, or water data? For which service, place, and period?
- Is this figure measured, modelled, or simply repeated from somewhere else?
- What information would help me make a better decision?

Write down one thing you learned and one question you still cannot answer.

## 2. Lighten

Make intentional choices that reduce unnecessary digital demand. Start with changes you control, then consider the defaults and systems that shape everyone's choices.

Possible actions:

- Use a video resolution appropriate to the screen and purpose.
- Unsubscribe from messages you do not read and review unnecessary stored copies.
- Use AI for tasks where it adds value; consider whether search, a calculator, or an existing document already meets the need.
- Remove unused website code and optimise media for the way people actually use it.
- Keep working devices useful through maintenance, repair, and software that supports them.

Choose one change and a way to check whether it helped.

Choose changes that preserve accessibility, privacy, security, and the purpose of the service. A lighter page that somebody cannot use has failed its job. Keep captions and accessible alternatives, and preserve records you need.

For help reporting the result, see [evidence and measurement](EVIDENCE.md).

## 3. Lead

Share what you learned and make it easier for others to act. Leadership can be a conversation, a lesson, a better product default, or a question in a purchasing decision.

Possible actions:

- Teach the physical footprint of digital services alongside other technology skills.
- Share a practical change with your family or colleagues.
- Ask suppliers for service-specific environmental information and their measurement methods.
- Propose a media budget, maintenance practice, or review process for your team.
- Publish an example others can reproduce, including its limits.

Involve someone who can use what you learned, and agree on a next action.

Individual habits matter within larger systems. Developers, institutions, and suppliers also control design, infrastructure, procurement, and disclosure. Use your influence where you have it.

## Repeat

After trying a change, ask what happened. Was the task still completed? Did the change introduce a barrier? What did you measure? What remains uncertain?

Bring those answers back to Learn. Keep useful changes, revise weak ones, and share what you discovered. The [worksheet](templates/action-plan.md) supports this cycle.
