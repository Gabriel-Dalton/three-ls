# The 3 Ls in practice

These are fictional scenarios to help you apply the framework. Choose an action that fits your circumstances and check the result.

## At home: video settings

**Learn:** you notice that a video service defaults to its highest resolution. Check the setting and consider what you need for the screen you use.

**Lighten:** try a lower resolution for casual viewing. Keep readable detail, captions, and the quality needed for the content.

**Lead:** show someone how to find the setting. Explain the practical choice without claiming a fixed carbon saving for each video.

**Review:** was the content still useful and comfortable to watch? Did the setting persist?

## In a classroom: technology literacy

**Learn:** ask students to trace a familiar online activity through their device, network, and a data centre. Discuss which parts they can observe and which are hidden.

**Lighten:** students choose one practical change, such as avoiding an unnecessary download or reusing an existing resource.

**Lead:** each student explains the change and one unresolved question to a classmate. Offer an offline activity and school-provided examples so participation does not require a personal device or account.

**Review:** can students explain the physical infrastructure and distinguish a measurement from an estimate?

## On a website: a lighter event page

**Learn:** a community group checks its event page on a phone and an older laptop. It records the resources loaded and asks users about barriers.

**Lighten:** the developer resizes oversized images, removes unused code, and loads a video when requested. Captions, text alternatives, and keyboard controls remain available.

**Lead:** the group documents how future editors should prepare images and test the page.

**Review:** compare transferred data under the same conditions and confirm people can still find event details and register. Report the observed change; use a documented model for any emissions estimate.

## At work: choosing an AI tool

**Learn:** a team identifies the tasks it needs help with and asks vendors for service-specific energy information, methods, and disclosure dates.

**Lighten:** the team tries AI on a task where it helps and uses existing tools for simpler work. It reviews answer quality and the effort spent correcting errors.

**Lead:** the buyer adds environmental disclosure questions to future supplier reviews and shares the responses with the team.

**Review:** did the tool meet the need? What information did suppliers provide? What remains unknown? Avoid turning absent disclosures into a precise environmental ranking.
